# Drift — Final Demo Voiceover

Use this as a natural voiceover over the recording. Keep the tone calm, clear, and conversational. You do not need to say every word perfectly.

## Opening — Landing page

“Hey, this is Drift.

Most watchlists show me a lot of price changes, but they do not really tell me which changes matter. Drift is built to answer a simpler question: what actually changed since I last looked?

The idea is simple on the surface, but there is a lot happening behind it. Drift learns what normal movement looks like for each stock, checks different signals, and brings the important ones to the top.”

## Starting questions

“Before entering the product, I can answer two quick questions about how I invest and how often I like to check my watchlist.

These questions do not give me investment advice. They just help set up my starting point.

Once both are answered, I can build my watchlist.”

## Entering the demo

“I’ll use the demo account so I can go straight into the product without a long setup.”

## Premade watchlists

“Here I can create a new watchlist in two ways.

I can start completely from scratch, or I can use one of Drift’s premade watchlists. There are ready-made groups for areas like Technology, Banking, Pharma, EVs, and other themes.

This is useful because I do not have to search for every stock one by one. I can start with a complete group and then change it to fit me.”

## Creating a personal watchlist

“For this demo, I’ll create a new watchlist from scratch.

I’ll call it My Signals.

Now I can add the companies I actually want to follow. I’ll search for Netflix, choose the verified result, and add it to this watchlist. I’ll also add Disney, and Uber if I want one more example.

So I can use Drift’s ready-made lists, or I can build a completely personal list.”

## Overview and watchlist switching

“Now My Signals is its own watchlist. I can switch between my different watchlists from the side rail.

The overview gives me the main picture: how many stocks I am tracking, the market context, when the data was refreshed, and how many normal movements Drift filtered out.

That filtering is important. Drift is doing work in the background so I do not have to scan every price change myself.”

## Attention feed

“When Drift finds something unusual, it brings it into the attention feed.

It does not treat every stock the same. A two-percent move can be normal for one stock and very unusual for another, so Drift compares each stock with its own normal behavior.

It also looks at volume, important price levels, the other stocks in my watchlist, and the wider market.”

## Calm state

“If everything is quiet, that is useful too.

It means Drift did not find a meaningful change, so it did not create another unnecessary alert. The goal is not more notifications. The goal is better attention.”

## Stock detail drawer

“When I open a stock, I can see the current number, the chart, and the exact reasons it appeared.

This is where the product becomes more than a simple price list. It tells me whether the move was unusual for this stock, whether volume changed, whether it is near an important level, or whether it moved differently from the rest of the market.

I can also add my own thesis, manage which watchlists contain the stock, open the full chart, or mark it as seen.”

## Personal baseline

“When I mark a stock as seen, Drift remembers where I was.

The next time I return, it can compare the stock from my last real visit instead of only comparing it with yesterday’s closing price.

That personal baseline is one of the details that makes the product feel more useful over time.”

## Charts and history

“The Charts view lets me look more closely at the movement of a stock.

The History view shows what Drift has surfaced before, so I can understand what happened over time instead of only seeing the latest number.”

## Beginner mode

“If the language feels too technical, I can turn on Beginner mode.

It makes the explanations easier to understand, but it does not change the signal, the score, or the data. It only changes the wording.”

## Drifty AI

“Drifty AI can also give me a short plain-English summary.

It helps me read the result faster, but it does not decide what gets flagged. The real rules and numbers are still visible underneath, so the alert stays explainable.”

## Closing

“So the full flow is simple: I can start with a premade watchlist or create my own, add the companies I care about, and come back later to see what actually changed.

The interface looks simple because the complicated work happens behind the scenes: fresh market data, stock-specific movement, watchlist comparisons, market context, history, and my own last-view baseline.

I’ll explain how the rules and the code work later through GitHub. For now, the important part is that every alert has a reason I can read.

That is Drift: less scanning, more useful attention.”
